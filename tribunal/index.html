About this data

  • Source. Market rent decisions published by the First-tier Tribunal on GOV.UK, decided on or after 1 May 2026. Checked daily. Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0.
  • Automated extraction. Rents, bedrooms, bills and outcomes are read from each decision by a rule-based parser and can be wrong. Rows marked “check” showed an inconsistency in the published decision. Always read the original decision before relying on a figure.
  • Case summaries. Select “Summary” on any row. Each summary is assembled from the figures and facts read from that decision, in fixed wording: the landlord’s proposal, how the Tribunal handled the case, its view of the market rent and any deductions where the decision states them, and the result. It does not reproduce the Tribunal’s reasoning, so read the decision for the full picture.
  • Monthly rents. Weekly, fortnightly and four-weekly rents are converted to a calendar month so cases can be compared. Hover over a rent to see the figure as stated.
  • Bills included. “Yes” means the decision records council tax, utilities, water or broadband as covered by the rent. “No” means it records the tenant as responsible. “Not stated” means the decision is silent.
  • Which cases are included. Cases decided under the Housing Act 1988 as amended by the Renters’ Rights Act. Under regulation 8 of S.I. 2026/421, a rent increase notice served before 1 May 2026 is still decided under the old rules, so the date the notice was served decides which cases are in. Where a decision gives no notice date, the Tribunal’s new case codes (MRA, MRB) are used. Challenges to a new tenancy’s starting rent under section 14(A1) are included too. Each case summary says why the case was included.
  • Rent payable. Under section 14ZB(5) the tenant pays the Tribunal’s open-market rent only if it is lower than the landlord’s proposal. Where the Tribunal’s figure is higher, the tracker shows the proposed rent as the rent set and gives the Tribunal’s figure in the summary.
  • Landlord. “Social” marks cases where the decision points to a housing association, council or charity.
  • Personal data. This tracker does not hold the names of tenants, landlords or their representatives.
